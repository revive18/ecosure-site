Storm Season Prep: Protecting Your Home from Mold Before the Rain Hits

Rainy season in Los Angeles and San Diego doesn't last long, but it doesn't need to — a single roof leak or flooded crawl space is enough to start a mold problem if it isn't dried out fast. Here's how to get ahead of it.

Short answer: Mold can start growing within 24-48 hours of water intrusion, so the window to prevent it is short. Before storms hit, check your roof, gutters, and grading for anything that could let water in or pool near your foundation. If water does get in, dry the area within a day or two using fans and dehumidifiers, and if it soaked into drywall, carpet, or insulation, or you're not sure it dried out completely, independent testing can confirm whether it's actually a problem before it becomes a bigger, more expensive one.

Before the Rain: A Quick Prep Checklist

  • Clear gutters and downspouts so water is directed away from your roofline and foundation instead of pooling.
  • Check your roof for damaged or missing shingles/tiles and have any obvious issues repaired before the first storm, not after.
  • Inspect window and door seals for gaps that let wind-driven rain in.
  • Check grading around your foundation — ground should slope away from the house, not toward it.
  • Look at crawl spaces and basements for existing dampness before it gets worse under sustained rain.

If Water Gets In

The clock matters more than almost anything else once water intrudes. Move quickly:

  • Stop the source if it's safe to do so, and remove standing water as soon as possible.
  • Run fans and a dehumidifier on the affected area — aim to have it fully dry within 24-48 hours.
  • Pull up wet carpet padding and remove soaked, porous materials that won't dry out in place.
  • Photograph the damage as you go, for insurance purposes.

For a fuller walkthrough of the first two days after a leak, see our guide on mold after water damage: what to do in the first 48 hours.

When to Bring in Independent Testing

Not every rain event needs a test — if water was minor and fully dried within a day or two, you're often fine. Testing is worth it when water sat for longer than that, soaked into drywall, insulation, or carpet, or when you notice a musty smell afterward and want a clear answer instead of a guess. It's also worth it if the affected area is now enclosed behind repaired drywall — once it's closed up, a visual check alone can't tell you what's happening inside the wall.

Frequently Asked Questions

How soon after a roof leak or flooding can mold start growing?

Mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours in the right conditions of moisture and warmth. That's why drying out affected materials quickly after a storm matters more than almost anything else you can do.

Should I test for mold right after a storm, or wait?

If water intrusion was minor and dried within 24-48 hours, testing may not be necessary. If water sat for longer, soaked into drywall or carpet, or you notice a musty smell afterward, independent testing gives you a clear answer instead of guessing.

Does homeowners insurance cover mold from storm damage?

It depends on your policy and how quickly the damage was addressed. Many policies cover mold that results from a sudden, covered event like storm damage, but exclude mold from long-term neglect. An independent test report can help document the timeline and extent of an issue for a claim.
