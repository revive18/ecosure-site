Why Glendale homes and buildings get mold

Glendale is a dense foothill city between the Verdugo Mountains and the LA basin. Glendale's tucked-in position against the Verdugos means still air and slow drying, so a leak in a condo or hillside home can stay wet long enough for mold to take hold.

Glendale mixes older single-family homes with a large stock of mid-rise condos and apartments, where shared walls and stacked plumbing spread water problems between units. The most common triggers we see in Glendale include shared-wall leaks in condos and apartments, hillside drainage against foundations, and limited airflow in dense multi-unit buildings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you serve all of Glendale?

Yes — we cover Glendale and its surrounding neighborhoods, including Adams Hill, Montrose, Rossmoyne, Verdugo Woodlands, and downtown Glendale, plus nearby Los Angeles communities. If you're not sure we reach your address, just call and ask.

What's the difference between a mold inspection and mold testing?

An inspection is the hands-on assessment — finding moisture and visible growth and locating the source. Testing is the lab analysis of samples. A complete job usually includes both, and we do them together.

What does the inspector actually look for?

Signs of past or active water intrusion: staining, warping, musty odor, elevated moisture readings, and high-risk spots like bathrooms, under sinks, around windows, and in HVAC systems.
