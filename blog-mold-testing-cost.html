How Much Does Mold Testing Cost in Los Angeles & San Diego? (2026)

If you're worried about mold, the first question is usually "what's this going to cost me?" Here's an honest breakdown of mold testing prices in Southern California, what drives them, and how to make sure you're paying for real answers — not a sales pitch.

The short answer

Nationally, a professional mold inspection typically runs about $300 to $650, with more complex jobs reaching $1,000+. Basic testing on its own often falls in the $250–$350 range, and sending samples to an accredited lab for analysis adds to that. In higher-cost metros like Los Angeles and San Diego, expect to be in the middle-to-upper part of that range.

The honest truth: the price depends on your specific property, so the only accurate number is a quote for your home. We're happy to give you one for free.

What drives the price

Four things move the number up or down:

  • Property size. A small condo needs fewer samples than a large multi-story house.
  • Number of samples. Each air or surface sample sent to the lab has a cost. More problem areas = more samples.
  • Type of testing. Standard air and surface sampling is the baseline; specialized tests (like ERMI or mycotoxin testing) cost more.
  • Access. Hard-to-reach areas like crawl spaces and attics can add time.

What about the cheap $10–$100 DIY kits?

You'll see mold test kits at the hardware store for $10 to $100. They're inexpensive for a reason: a settling dish left on a counter will grow something in almost any home, because mold spores are naturally in the air everywhere. Without a trained inspector, outdoor baseline comparison, and accredited lab analysis, DIY kits tend to create false alarms — or false comfort. For a decision that affects your health or a real estate transaction, they're not a substitute for professional testing.

Why "free mold inspections" can cost you more

Many companies advertise free mold inspections. The catch is that most of them also sell remediation — the cleanup work. The more mold they find, the more work they can sell you. That's a built-in conflict of interest, and it's exactly why an independent tester exists.

Eco Sure Testing does testing only. We never perform remediation, so we have no reason to over-report mold. You pay for an honest, defensible result — and if it turns out you don't have a problem, we'll tell you that too.

How to avoid overpaying

Ask any mold tester three questions: Do you do remediation too? (An independent tester will say no.) Is the lab accredited? (It should be.) And will I get a written report with the actual lab data? (You should.) If the answers are good, you're paying for real information you can act on.
