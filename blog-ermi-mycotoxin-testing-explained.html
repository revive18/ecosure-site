ERMI and Mycotoxin Testing Explained

You've probably seen these terms searching for mold testing options. Here's what they actually measure, what they're genuinely useful for, and where their limits are — explained plainly, without the upsell.

Short answer: ERMI is a dust-sample DNA test that scores a property against 36 mold species; it was developed as a research tool, not a certified stand-alone diagnostic for individual homes, so it's most useful alongside standard air and surface testing, not instead of it. Mycotoxin urine testing measures your body, not your home, and lacks validated reference ranges — a high result doesn't tell you where an exposure happened or confirm your property is the source. Standard air, surface, and moisture testing remains the most reliable starting point for most situations.

What ERMI Actually Measures

ERMI stands for Environmental Relative Moldiness Index. A technician collects a dust sample — usually vacuumed from carpet or flooring — and a lab runs qPCR (a DNA analysis method) to identify and quantify 36 specific mold species, then combines the results into a single numeric score. It was originally developed by the EPA as a research tool for large-scale studies comparing water-damaged and non-water-damaged homes, not as a certified diagnostic test for an individual property.

That distinction matters. ERMI can flag mold species and relative levels that standard air sampling might miss, since it captures settled dust that has accumulated over time rather than a snapshot of what's airborne that day. But because it wasn't built or validated as a stand-alone pass/fail test for one house, most independent testers treat it as a useful supplement to — not a replacement for — air quality, surface, and moisture testing.

What Mycotoxin Testing Measures — and Doesn't

Mycotoxins are compounds some molds produce under certain conditions. Testing comes in two very different forms, and it's worth keeping them separate:

  • Environmental (dust or surface) mycotoxin testing looks for mycotoxins in the property itself — closer in spirit to air and surface testing, though the science and lab standardization here is still evolving.
  • Urine mycotoxin testing measures your body, not the property. Some functional medicine practitioners offer it, but mainstream toxicology and allergy organizations point out there are no established reference ranges for what counts as a "normal" or "elevated" result, and a positive result can't tell you when, where, or from what source an exposure occurred — including from food, which is a common source of mycotoxin exposure unrelated to your home.

If a practitioner has recommended urine mycotoxin testing for a health concern, that's a conversation to have with them — it's outside what property-based testing can confirm either way. What we can tell you is whether your property itself shows elevated mold levels through standard, well-established methods.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ERMI better than a standard air quality test?

Not automatically. ERMI analyzes settled dust for DNA from 36 mold species and produces a single index score, which can be useful supporting information, but it was developed as a research tool, not a certified stand-alone diagnostic for individual homes. Most independent testers use it to complement, not replace, air and surface sampling.

Does a high mycotoxin urine test mean my house has toxic mold?

Not by itself. Urine mycotoxin testing is used by some practitioners, but mainstream toxicology and allergy organizations note there are no validated reference ranges for these tests and they cannot reliably identify where or when an exposure happened. A urine test result is not a substitute for testing the property itself.

Should I ask for ERMI or mycotoxin testing at my property?

It depends on your situation. If you have specific health concerns, standard air, surface, and moisture testing is usually the right starting point, and ERMI dust sampling can be added for extra data in ambiguous cases. We'll tell you honestly whether either test adds value for your specific property rather than selling you every option available.
